\begin{abstract}
Voice interfaces can broaden access to digital health support, yet conversational agents that touch clinical context introduce risks of privacy leakage, missing consent, hallucinated advice, and uncontrolled tool use.
We present Zorba Health, an open-source microservice platform that combines a LiveKit-backed voice agent, a Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool gateway, a FHIR-compatible retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) path with citations, and an append-only audit and consent service.
We release a synthetic evaluation corpus of 16 patients (646 filtered FHIR resources), 25 gold QA items, 22 exact and 22 paraphrase escalation utterances, and 8 consent scenarios.
Offline type-aware lexical Recall@3 $= 1.00$.
Exact-phrase escalation F1 $= 1.00$, but paraphrase F1 $= 0.29$ (combined F1 $= 0.71$).
Consent-gate e2e deny/allow/regrant for health-record access passes, with a documented gap for third-party model consent.
A live OpenAI RAG evaluation with answer-string-required scoring yields answerable accuracy $= 0.85$ (17/20), citation presence $= 1.00$, unanswerable safe refusal $= 1.00$ (5/5), mean latency $1.67$\,s (p95 $2.43$\,s).
We argue that separating voice runtime, tool policy, clinical retrieval, and compliance logging is a practical path toward auditable healthcare agents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Telephone and voice channels remain highly accessible for patients who struggle with app-centric portals.
Large language models (LLMs) enable natural dialogue, but na\"ive deployments can invent clinical facts, bypass consent, omit audit trails, and couple the agent directly to sensitive stores.
The Model Context Protocol (MCP) standardizes tool exposure but cannot enforce consent or authorization at the protocol layer; implementors must build those controls~\cite{mcp2024}.
WHO guidance on large multi-modal models for health similarly emphasizes governance and careful escalation~\cite{who2024lmm}.

\textbf{Problem.}
How can a voice healthcare assistant be engineered so that tool use, record retrieval, and AI summarization are consent-gated, auditable, grounded in patient-specific FHIR context, and reproducibly evaluable?

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1) an open-source voice healthcare microservice architecture;
(2) a controlled MCP tool gateway that materializes the consent flows the MCP specification leaves to implementors;
(3) a FHIR-compatible RAG pipeline with citations;
(4) a safety, consent, and audit framework;
(5) a reproducible synthetic evaluation package including live RAG, consent-gate, and paraphrase safety tests.

\section{Related Work}
Relational agents targeted accessible care~\cite{bickmore2010relational}, while modern medical LLMs encode clinical knowledge~\cite{singhal2023medpalm,nori2023gpt4medicine}.
Polaris~\cite{mukherjee2024polaris} is a proprietary multi-agent voice constellation evaluated by over 1{,}100 U.S.\ licensed nurses; Zorba Health instead prioritizes an open-source, consent-gated MCP architecture without clinical-parity claims.
HL7 FHIR R4 and Synthea~\cite{walonoski2018synthea,hl7fhirr4,mandl2016fhir} enable synthetic EHR research without PHI; MedAgentBench~\cite{jiang2025medagentbench} adds a FHIR-compliant interactive agent benchmark.
Retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{lewis2020rag}, medical RAG benchmarks~\cite{es2023ragas,xiong2024medicalrag}, Almanac~\cite{zakka2024almanac}, and FHIR-RAG-MEDS~\cite{kabak2026fhirragmeds} quantify clinical grounding.
Tool-using LLMs~\cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer}, AgentClinic~\cite{schmidgall2024agentclinic}, and HealthBench~\cite{arora2025healthbench} motivate interactive evaluation beyond static QA.
MCP~\cite{mcp2024} standardizes tool exposure; early healthcare MCP prototypes~\cite{shehab2025agenticai,elsayed2025mcpai} document compliance layers, while Hou~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{hou2025mcplandscape} systematize MCP threats.
HIPAA~\cite{hipaa1996} and WHO LMM guidance~\cite{who2024lmm} motivate consent and audit.
Crisis-detection benchmarks~\cite{deng2025psycrisisbench,arnaiz2026crisis} show LLMs can reach high F1 on explicit suicidal ideation yet still fail on indirect signals---framing our paraphrase safety collapse.

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{Consent-Aware RAG}
The pipeline validates inputs; checks \texttt{HEALTH\_RECORD\_ACCESS}; embeds the query; performs patient-scoped vector search; optionally summarizes only over retrieved chunks; audits events; returns citations.

\subsection{Safety Escalation}
A deterministic phrase classifier triggers hospital-visible escalation for emergency-like utterances.

\subsection{Standards Alignment}
Application-level consent types (\texttt{HEALTH\_RECORD\_ACCESS}, \texttt{THIRD\_PARTY\_MODEL\_PROCESSING}) are complementary to the FHIR R4 Consent resource~\cite{hl7fhirconsent}; mapping typed grants to FHIR Consent is future work.
SMART App Launch v2.2 scopes~\cite{smartapplaunch22} authorize FHIR API clients; the MCP gateway sits above SMART-style scopes as a tool-mediation and audit boundary for agent invocations, not as a replacement for OAuth/SMART.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Setup}
We report: (1) lexical Recall@3; (2) live OpenAI RAG with answer-string-required audit; (3) consent-gate stub e2e; (4) exact vs paraphrase safety; (5) Go unit/contract tests.

\subsection{Results}
Lexical existence oracle and type-aware Recall@3 are both $1.00$.
Live RAG answerable accuracy is $0.85$ (17/20) with citation presence $1.00$ and safe refusal $1.00$ on 5 LDL probes (mean $1667$\,ms, p95 $2432$\,ms).
Error analysis: allergy misses in answer text (3) and one medication answer that incorrectly included penicillin (an allergy) alongside albuterol.
Exact escalation F1 $= 1.00$; paraphrase F1 $= 0.29$; combined F1 $= 0.71$ (Table~\ref{tab:safety}).
Consent deny/allow/regrant for \texttt{HEALTH\_RECORD\_ACCESS} passes; third-party model consent remains a known RAG-pipeline gap.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Separating MCP and audit from the voice runtime creates a single policy enforcement point.
Relative to Hou~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{hou2025mcplandscape}, the gateway mitigates unauthorized tool invocation, weak accountability, and unmediated PHI access, but does not yet address installer spoofing or cryptographic tool-manifest signing.
Relative to Polaris~\cite{mukherjee2024polaris}, we trade proprietary constellation training for an auditable open architecture.
Limitations include modest live-RAG gold size, paraphrase brittleness of phrase safety (motivating LLM/embedding classifiers~\cite{deng2025psycrisisbench,arnaiz2026crisis}), incomplete third-party consent enforcement, lack of clinician-rated groundedness, and absence of MedAgentBench-style interactive evaluation~\cite{jiang2025medagentbench}.
Synthetic FHIR under-represents messy clinical narratives.
Aligning typed consents with FHIR Consent~\cite{hl7fhirconsent} and composing MCP mediation with SMART scopes~\cite{smartapplaunch22} are interoperability priorities.

\section{Conclusion}
Zorba Health provides an open, consent-aware architecture for voice healthcare agents with FHIR-grounded RAG and MCP-mediated tools, plus a reproducible synthetic evaluation package with honest safety stress results.
Future work includes paraphrase-robust escalation, third-party consent enforcement, FHIR Consent / SMART alignment, MedAgentBench and HealthBench-style evaluation, Synthea-scale gold labels, and clinician review.
